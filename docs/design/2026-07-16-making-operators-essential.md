# Design brainstorm: making − / × / ÷ actually essential

**Date:** 2026-07-16 · **Status:** ideas for discussion (nothing built yet)

## The problem (from playtesting)

Multi-target progression and the −/×/÷ operators shipped, but playing it revealed the real
issue: **addition + splitters is a doubling machine.** Split a belt of 5s into an adder → 10s;
chain it → any number, cheaply. So multiplication (and eventually division) never becomes
*necessary* — the extra operators feel decorative. Frank's instinct: be deliberate about the
resources (e.g. give **primes** and make targets out of them), and/or reshape the game
(discrete puzzles, or a fresh map per operator "era").

Below is a brainstorm (generated by a Fable design pass) of six directions that each attack the
"× is optional" problem a different way, then a recommendation, then bolt-on twists. An
**Engineering notes** appendix (grounded in our actual code) follows.

---

## Six directions

### 1. The Prime Foundry
Every level's deposits are **only primes** ({2,3,5,7,11}); every target is a composite built
from them — you're constructing factor trees out of belts. Splitting a belt of 3s into a `×` is
literally a "squaring machine."
- **Makes × essential:** 45 = 3×3×5 is 2 machines; the additive route from {3,5} is ~4 machines
  of spaghetti. Addition still *works* (never stuck) but is visibly miserable as targets grow.
- **+ returns** for "almost-factorable" targets (33 = 2×3×5 + 3); **−/÷** star in boss primes
  (29 = 5×5 + 2+2).
- **Ramp:** 2-factor (6, 12) → 3-factor (30, 90) → repeated factors (36 = 6×6) → factor+remainder
  (33) → boss primes (29). **Risk:** deposits become part of the puzzle statement (less "mine
  whatever"); keep early targets *odd* (45, 63, 75) so adder-doubling can't sneak back in.

### 2. Era Islands
A gentler take on "reset per operator": don't wipe — each era unlocks a **new island** (curated
region of the already-unbounded world) built around that op; old islands keep humming.
- **Multiplication Isle** {2,3,7} → 42 (6×7), 126; **Division Isle** {96,2,3} → 8 (you can't add
  your way *down*); **Archipelago capstone** 2025 = 3×3×9×... from {3,5} ferried via tunnels.
- **Preserves her factory** (your reset idea's goal) while still feeling fresh. **Risk:** more
  authoring; needs per-region deposit spawning (already on the roadmap as procedural deposits).

### 3. Counted Veins
Deposits have stock ("5 ×6 left") that miners consume and that slowly regrows. Addition burns ore
∝ target value; × amplifies value, so it sips ore.
- **Makes × essential:** target 100 from {5,2}: adding needs twenty 5s (impossible); 5×5×2×2 uses
  two 5s. The ticking counter makes the *reason* visceral ("adding eats all my fives!").
- Teaches the deepest idea here: **× creates magnitude, + only accumulates it.** **Risk:** biggest
  engine change (stock/regen/UI/save); softens the infinite "watch it hum" feel — tune regen.

### 4. The Breaker Yard
Invert it: the quarry delivers **boulders** (60, 84, 96); orders want **pebbles** (12, 6, 2). You
build a demolition line.
- **÷ by construction:** 84 ÷ 7 = 12, ÷2 = 6. Floor division becomes a *feature* (100÷7=14, teaches
  quotients). Round-trip pairs: one level builds 84 from primes, the next breaks it back down.
- **Risk:** narrow as a whole game (division fatigue) — best as the *division era's* content.

### 5. Number Golf
Keep today's sandbox; add a **par** per level ("fill the hub with ≤3 machines"). Filling the bar
completes it *any* way (never stuck); meeting par earns a gold gear → cosmetics.
- **Makes × rewarding:** 64 from {2,3} in par-3 is 2×2×4×4; the adder route needs 5. **Risk:** ×
  is essential only *for the reward*, not progress — evaporates if she doesn't chase gears.
  Cheapest to build, though.

### 6. Picky Eaters
Hubs become creatures wanting **properties**, not exact numbers: "multiples of 7", "even & >20",
"square numbers."
- **Makes × essential:** the only guaranteed way to a multiple of 7 is to bolt a ×7 on the end;
  Squarrel teaches split-into-× squaring. Teaches number-sense vocabulary (multiple/even/square).
- **Risk:** loses the crisp "I hit exactly 45!" moment; needs a kid-readable icon language; hub
  logic changes from equality to predicate.

---

## Recommendation (Fable's, and I agree)

**Build the Prime Foundry, staged as Era Islands, with Number Golf pars as a mastery layer.**
- The multiplication era = a Prime Foundry island ({2,3,5,7}, odd factor-rich targets); the
  division era = a Breaker Yard island; the existing addition levels are the first island,
  unchanged.
- **Essentiality comes from deposit curation + target choice, not hard limits** — addition stays
  possible (never-stuck survives), but on a prime island with 45/90/225 the × route is 2–3 tidy
  machines and the + route is visibly absurd. Pars celebrate the clever route rather than forcing it.
- **Smallest engine delta for the biggest fix:** levels/deposits/targets are already data-driven,
  and the world is already unbounded/chunked, so "an island" ≈ "spawn this curated deposit set in
  this region" — work we'd do anyway for procedural deposits. Her factory never gets wiped.
- **First build slice:** one multiplication island, four levels — **6, 45, 36, 90** — plus a par
  badge. That alone answers "why does × exist?" in one session.

## Twists (bolt onto any direction)
1. **Remainder crumbs** — the `÷` machine gets a second output port that drops the remainder as an
   item (17÷5 → a 3 out front, a 2 out the side). Euclidean division made visible; highest
   delight-per-line here.
2. **Boss factor-tree hubs** — a hub that wants the *pieces*: defeat 360 by delivering 2,2,2,3,3,5,
   each lighting up a factor tree. Works forwards (mine primes) or as a Breaker Yard finale.
3. **The squeeze plot** — some levels give a small walled build area; additive sprawl won't fit, the
   compact × solution will. Nearly free (a region bound + fence), and it makes splitters/tunnels
   into puzzle assets. Framed warmly ("yard's full — can a multiplier shrink this?").
4. *(Freebie)* confetti when an item's value is exactly 100 or 1000.

## Engineering notes (grounded in the current code)

Rough cost of each, given what already exists (`src/content/levels.ts` + `operations.ts` are
data; `sim/tick.ts` win check is `it.value === b.target`; world is sparse/chunked/unbounded):

| Direction | Where it lands | Rough effort |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Prime Foundry** | Pure data edit to `LEVELS` (prime `grantNodes`, odd composite targets). Add a test that the intended factor route exists. **Zero engine change.** | **Low** — do-able in one sitting |
| **2. Era Islands** | Per-region deposit spawning in `worldgen.ts`/`world.ts` (procedural deposits, already "Next") + island authoring; maybe camera "go to new island." | Medium |
| **3. Counted Veins** | New `ResourceNode` stock/regen state, miner consumption, HUD counter, **save version bump + migration.** | High |
| **4. Breaker Yard** | Mostly content (big deposits, small targets); `÷` already works. Pairs well as the division island. | Low–Med |
| **5. Number Golf** | Count placed machines per level (data `par`), a gold-gear reward + cosmetics UI. No sim change. | Low–Med |
| **6. Picky Eaters** | Change hub matching from equality to a predicate (`sim/tick.ts` + `TargetBuilding`); icon language for properties. | Medium |
| **Twist: remainder crumbs** | Second output cell on the operator for `÷`; sim emit + render a small side item. | Medium |
| **Twist: factor-tree boss** | Multi-slot hub bar (deliver a *set* of values). | Medium |
| **Twist: squeeze plot** | A per-level build-bounds rectangle enforced in `place.ts` + a fence sprite. | Low |

**My take:** start with **#1 Prime Foundry as a pure `LEVELS` data experiment** — it's the cheapest
possible change (no engine work, no save bump), it directly implements your primes idea, and it will
immediately tell us whether "× matters now." If it lands, grow it into Era Islands (which also gives
us the procedural-deposits work) and sprinkle Number-Golf pars. Keep the current addition ladder as
"Island 1." The **squeeze plot** and **confetti** twists are cheap wins whenever.
